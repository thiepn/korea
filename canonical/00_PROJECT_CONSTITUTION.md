# KOREA — A Complete History and Civilization
## Project Constitution v1.0
### FINAL CANONICAL — LOCKED

This Constitution governs the architecture, source documents, research workflow, and future production of the project **KOREA — A Complete History and Civilization**.

It is the highest-authority project document. No later chat, Part workflow, research pass, writing pass, or production decision may silently contradict it.

---

# 1. Project Mission

The project aims to construct an unusually comprehensive, evidence-conscious history and civilization of Korea from geological and prehistoric foundations to the contemporary period.

Its scope includes, where historically relevant:

- archaeology and material culture;
- geography, environment, climate, resources, and natural hazards;
- population history, migration, genetics, and ethnogenesis;
- language, writing, literacy, education, and knowledge systems;
- state formation, monarchy, government, bureaucracy, law, and administration;
- warfare, military institutions, diplomacy, frontiers, and international relations;
- agriculture, land, taxation, labor, commerce, finance, industry, and technology;
- status, slavery/dependency, class, family, gender, childhood, aging, disability, and everyday life;
- religion, ritual, philosophy, intellectual life, literature, music, visual art, architecture, and performance;
- medicine, disease, public health, nutrition, demography, and care;
- regional and local history, including provinces, cities, villages, islands, borderlands, and local identities;
- migration, diaspora, refugees, transnational networks, and Koreans abroad;
- colonialism, resistance, nationalism, socialism, collaboration, coercion, and memory;
- North Korea, South Korea, inter-Korean relations, and global Korea;
- historiography, source criticism, public memory, and competing interpretations.

The project is not limited to political or dynastic history. It must remain a history of **civilization, institutions, society, lived experience, material worlds, ideas, and historical interpretation**.

# 2. Exact Architectural Invariant

The project uses a fixed decimal hierarchy:

1. **10 Parts**
2. **10 Chapters per Part = 100 Chapters**
3. **10 Subchapters per Chapter = 1,000 Subchapters**
4. **10 Sections per Subchapter = 10,000 Sections**
5. **10 terminal Subsections per Section = 100,000 terminal Subsections**

Formula:

\[
10 \times 10 \times 10 \times 10 \times 10 = 100{,}000
\]

This exact hierarchy is non-negotiable unless the Project Constitution itself is explicitly versioned and amended.

No future workflow may:
- reduce the number of levels;
- collapse levels for convenience;
- create 8, 9, 11, or another number of children at a locked decimal level;
- introduce hidden numbered levels;
- replace the decimal system with a different hierarchy.

# 3. Canonical Identifier System

Identifiers are permanent structural addresses.

Examples:

- `VI` = Part VI
- `VI.4` = Chapter VI.4
- `VI.4.3` = Subchapter VI.4.3
- `VI.4.3.6` = Section VI.4.3.6
- `VI.4.3.6.8` = terminal Subsection VI.4.3.6.8

Roman numerals are used for Parts.

Once an identifier is locked, it should remain stable. Reassigning a locked identifier is a structural revision and requires explicit change control.

   - geography;
   - source strategy.

7. **Section architectures once audited and locked**

# 9. Recurrence and Duplication Rule

A subject may recur only when its **historical function or analytical question changes**.

Legitimate recurrence examples:

- Donghak as religion → Donghak as 1894 mass political movement.
- Hangul creation → vernacular expansion → colonial language movement → North/South linguistic divergence.
- Kim Jong Il's rise → actual succession and rule.
- Jang Bogo as maritime-commercial power → consequences for Silla succession politics.

Forbidden duplication:

> same event + same historical question + same evidence + same explanation in multiple canonical units.

When context belongs elsewhere, later prose should cross-reference the canonical owner rather than rebuild the same discussion.

---

# 10. Part-Boundary Rule

At chronological transitions:

- the **earlier** unit generally owns:
  - collapse;
  - final war;
  - termination;
  - surrender;
  - end of a political order.

- the **later** unit generally owns:
  - aftermath;
  - incorporation;
  - reconstruction;
  - institutionalization;
  - consequences;
  - the new historical order.

Specific master boundary rules override this general principle.

---

# 11. Evidence and Epistemic Discipline

Historical claims must be conceptually separable into:

1. **Established evidence**
2. **Probable reconstruction**
3. **Disputed interpretation**
4. **Traditional account**
5. **Mythological account**

This distinction is mandatory where uncertainty matters.

Especially sensitive areas include:

- prehistoric chronology and migration;
- ethnogenesis and ancient DNA interpretation;
- Dangun;
- Gija/Jizi;
- Gojoseon location and chronology;
- Han commanderies;
- early state origins;
- ancient Korea–Japan relations;
- Gaya;
- Balhae identity;
- nationalist historiography;
- colonial administrative/statistical claims;
- collaboration and resistance;
- North Korean official historiography;
- defector testimony;
- contemporary politically contested events.

The project must prefer explicit uncertainty over false certainty.

# 12. Source Criticism Rule

No source type is automatically neutral.

Examples requiring perspective control:

- dynastic chronicles;
- royal annals;
- court memorials;
- foreign dynastic histories;
- colonial Government-General records;
- nationalist histories;
- missionary accounts;
- intelligence records;
- party/state propaganda;
- diplomatic records;
- memoirs;
- newspapers;
- statistics;
- oral testimony;
- defector testimony;
- contemporary government statements.

The future writing must ask:

> Who produced this evidence, for what purpose, from what position, and with what limitations?

# 16. Contemporary History Rule

Part X is a living-history Part.

Its **architecture is fixed**, but factual content may be updated over time.

A new:

- president;
- election;
- diplomatic crisis;
- technological development;
- demographic change;
- cultural trend;
- North Korean policy;
- inter-Korean development

does not automatically justify architectural revision.

New developments should first be absorbed into the existing canonical structure.

Only genuine long-term structural inadequacy justifies a versioned architecture revision.

# 18. Prohibition on Artificial Filler

The decimal structure is exact, but content must remain substantive.

> repartition the parent subject until ten genuinely substantive units exist.

- micro-chronological fragments;
- weak biographies;

solely to satisfy the number ten.
