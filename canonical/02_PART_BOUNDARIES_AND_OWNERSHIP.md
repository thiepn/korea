# Part Boundaries and Ownership v1.0
## FINAL CANONICAL — LOCKED

This document defines **who owns what** across Part, Chapter, and recurring-theme boundaries.

It exists to prevent the future ten Part chats from duplicating the same event, process, institution, or interpretation.

# 1. Governing Rule

At a chronological transition:

- the earlier unit normally owns **collapse, final war, surrender, termination, or end of an order**;
- the later unit normally owns **aftermath, incorporation, reconstruction, institutionalization, consequences, or the new order**.

Specific rules below override the general rule.

# 2. Cross-Part Boundaries

## I → II
**Part I owns:** prehistory, protohistory, state-formation models, and the threshold of written evidence.  
**Part II owns:** Gojoseon and other identifiable early states/polities.

**Critical rule:** `I.10` must not become a hidden Gojoseon chapter. `II.1` owns Gojoseon.

## II → III
**Part II owns:** formation of Goguryeo, Baekje, Silla, and emerging Gaya polities through approximately c. 300 CE.  
**Part III owns:** the mature fourth-century and later Three Kingdoms/Gaya interstate order.

## III → IV
**Part III owns:** the wars of unification, collapse of Baekje/Goguryeo, restoration movements, Tang protectorates, and the Silla–Tang war through the settlement around c. 676.  
**Part IV owns:** demographic, political, territorial, and social consequences of conquest; postwar integration; Silla–Balhae order.

## IV → V
**Part IV owns:** Wang Geon's rise, coalition, campaigns, reunification, and the immediate settlement of 936.  
**Part V owns:** consolidation and government of an already reunified Goryeo.

## V → VI
**Part V owns:** late Goryeo reform, Wihwa Island, revolutionary transition, and end of Goryeo.  
**Part VI owns:** proclamation and construction of the new Joseon order.

## VI → VII
**Part VI owns:** the Imjin War through the end of hostilities in 1598.  
**Part VII owns:** postwar devastation, reconstruction, military/fiscal recovery, and the Manchu challenge.

## VII → VIII
**Part VII owns:** external pressure and the coercive road to opening through the 1875 Un'yō/Ganghwa crisis.  
**Part VIII owns:** the Treaty of Ganghwa of 1876 and operation of the treaty-port system.

## VIII → IX
**Part VIII owns:** Japanese surrender and final collapse of colonial rule.  
**Part IX owns:** liberation politics, political vacuum, occupation, people's committees, competing postcolonial authority, and division.

## IX → X
**Part IX owns:** the old Cold War diplomatic order through joint UN membership in 1991.  
**Part X owns:** the 1991 Basic Agreement, Denuclearization Declaration, and the post-Cold-War inter-Korean framework.

# 4. Developmental Recurrence Rule

Recurring subjects are legitimate when their **historical function changes**.

Examples:
- Hangul: invention → vernacular expansion → colonial language movement → North/South divergence.
- Donghak: religious emergence → 1894 political/social movement.
- Jang Bogo: maritime system → political consequences.
- Kim Jong Il: pre-1991 succession preparation → post-1994 leadership consolidation.

Forbidden duplication:
**same event + same question + same evidence + same explanation** in multiple canonical units.

# 5. Cross-Reference Rule

When background belongs elsewhere:
- summarize only what is needed for orientation;
- cite/cross-reference the canonical owner;
- do not rebuild the entire explanation.
