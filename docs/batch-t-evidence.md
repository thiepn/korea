## Wiman Joseon and Han evidence

1. [National Institute of Korean History, annotated Shiji 115](https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_001_0050): Wiman's legacy, external-subject agreement, blocked envoys, She He, Han military commanders, Ugeo's murder and the conquest of Wanggeom. The imperial writer's account is primary but perspective-bound; there are no equivalent extant Joseon court annals.
2. [NIKH ancient state analysis](https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_003_0030_0010_0010_0030): migration, Yan identity, social structure, debate over material culture and conventional chronology.
3. [NIKH Han commanderies text and commentary](https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_001_0080): the Canghai experiment, 109–108 BCE war, Han's four commandery plan, variant 107 date for Xuantu and 82/75 BCE changes.
4. [NIKH account of commandery changes](https://contents.history.go.kr/front/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_004_0030_0020_0040_0020): distinctions between proclaimed control and local resistance.
5. [NIKH exchange archaeology](https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/km/view.do?levelId=km_008_0030_0010_0020): circulation of objects, uneven geographical distribution and trade interpretation.

**Critical boundaries:** Do not read later nationalities into Wiman's Yan connection; the ethnic composition of followers is unknown. The location of Paesu, Wanggeom and some early districts is disputed. Large numbers such as Namnyeo's claimed following are not independently registered populations. The Han chronicle's claims of obstruction and submission do not directly preserve Ugeo's viewpoint. Coins, mirrors and iron objects establish contact but do not by themselves establish political jurisdiction.

## Colonial state evidence

1. [National Archives Korea, Government-General administration](https://theme.archives.go.kr/next/place/outline.do?flag=08): 29 August 1910 installation, 30 September ordinance, October implementation and major 1919, 1937, 1943 reorganisations.
2. [NIKH study of Government-General powers](https://db.history.go.kr/diachronic/level.do?levelId=nh_047_0010): exceptional authority of the governor-general and military appointments.
3. [NIKH colonial policing institutional history](https://contents.history.go.kr/front/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_047_0020_0010): protectorate origins, combined gendarmerie-police functions, changes of 1919.
4. [NIKH cultural rule chronology](https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/kc/view.do?code=kc_age_40&levelId=kc_i403000): 1919 reform after mass protest, selective accommodation and sustained security control.
5. [Academy of Korean Studies, colonial period](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0047318): unequal colonial authority, changing administration and opposition.
6. [NIKH police history](https://db.history.go.kr/modern/level.do?levelId=hdsr_005_0020_0020_0020): policing in civil and military form, vigilance around educational/religious organisations.

**Critical boundaries:** A colonial ordinance is evidence of authorized policy, not uniform enforcement, equal rights or popular consent. Police reports reflect the interests and coercive powers of police agencies; suspected affiliations and named informants need corroboration. Changes under cultural rule did not grant national self-government. Wartime labour, military mobilisation and name changes involved distinct dated measures: do not treat forms or apparent compliance as freely chosen ideological allegiance. Ordinary Korean administrative work and deliberate participation in repression require differentiated evaluation.
