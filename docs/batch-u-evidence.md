## Gaya — source groups, primary-source limits, and disputed reconstruction

- [National Museum of Korea, Gaya Confederacy permanent gallery](https://www.museum.go.kr/ENG/contents/E0201030700.do?showHallId=760&showroomCode=DM0007): material evidence of iron ingots, weapons, pottery and Gaya regional cemeteries at Daeseong-dong, Malisan, Okjeon and Jisan-dong. Museum interpretations are institutional secondary syntheses, not contemporary political records.
- [UNESCO, Gaya Tumuli](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1666/): 2023 inscription of seven cemeteries, their independent centres and shared cultural forms. UNESCO describes multiple autonomous polities, not an ancient single constitution or proof of equal military power in every decade.
- [UNESCO, maps and component sites](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1666/maps/): sites at Gimhae, Haman, Hapcheon, Goryeong, Goseong, Namwon and Changnyeong. Archaeological ownership and boundaries are site-specific and must not be conflated with modern national boundaries.
- [Academy of Korean Studies, Gaya](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0000257): terminology, regional powers, the 400 campaign and annexation process. Its historical synthesis can conflict in detail with other reconstructions; represent contested claims as such.
- [Academy of Korean Studies, Daegaya](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0013970): distinction between Geumgwan surrender in **532** and Goryeong Daegaya conquest in **562**, plus varying reference names in the `Samguk Sagi`, `Nihon Shoki`, and other later histories.

**Primary-source caveats:** Gwanggaeto's stele was composed as a Goguryeo royal victory statement; the `Samguk Sagi` is a much later Goryeo compilation; the `Nihon Shoki` represents eighth-century Japanese court memory. Their language, dates and intended audiences differ. A foreign literary reference to `Imna/Mimana` does **not** establish a continuous Wa/Japanese colonial administration in Gaya. The Suro founding narrative and year **42 CE** are later dynastic tradition, not independently verified annual history. Burials, weapons and imported pottery support political differentiation, elite display, craft and contact, not a named ruler's exact tomb, individual battle experience, ethnic identity or sovereign jurisdiction. Regional and temporal differences should remain explicit.

## Unified Silla — source groups and archaeological conservation

- [National Museum of Korea, Unified Silla gallery](https://vcm.museum.go.kr/ENG/contents/E0201030900.do?showHallId=760&showroomCode=DM0034): Buddhist urns, sculpture, roof tiles, glazed wares and networks of exchange. Exhibit summaries compress major regional variation.
- [Academy of Korean Studies, Wonhyo](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0040908): 617–686, extensive scholastic writing and `hwajaeng`; biography and later anecdote must not be treated as equally direct evidence.
- [Academy of Korean Studies, Uisang](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0043250): 625–702, study under Zhiyan, movement to Tang in 661 and return in 670; separate doctrinal works from later monastic biography.
- [Academy of Korean Studies, Hwaeom tradition](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0064815): historical reception and transmission beyond the founder.
- [National Museum of Korea, Gameunsa reliquary](https://www.museum.go.kr/ENG/contents/E0403000000.do?relicId=2481&schM=relic_represent_view): securely contextualized royal-associated 682 pagoda and precious metal reliquaries.
- [Academy of Korean Studies, Nine Mountain Seon Schools](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0028926): different foundation dates, late-Silla to early-Goryeo lineages, provincial patronage.
- [National Museum of Korea, Yeomgeo stupa](https://vcm.museum.go.kr/JPN/contents/E0403000000.do?relicId=4316&schM=relic_represent_view): dated 844 Seon memorial and tangible teacher-lineage evidence.
- [Academy of Korean Studies, Hyangchal](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0062985), [Hyangga](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0062873), [Samguk Yusa](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0069669): character-based Korean notation, transmission through later manuscripts, and a **selective** corpus of surviving poems, not a census of all Silla literary production.
- [Academy of Korean Studies, mathematics doctor](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0026391): Gukhak founded in **682**, mathematics specialist role recorded from **717**. Later technologies must not be projected backward without dated evidence.
- [UNESCO, Seokguram Grotto and Bulguksa Temple](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/736): eighth-century sculpture and building, but **Seokguram was dismantled and rebuilt in 1913–1915**, with further intervention in the 1960s; Bulguksa wooden structures have been repaired and rebuilt. Distinguish original material, later restoration and current appearance.

**Interpretive caveats:** Doctrinal argument about oneness, mutual interpenetration or non-obstruction is Buddhist thought, not evidence for a specific secular constitution. The famous skull-water account of Wonhyo and biographical anecdotes about donors are **transmitted stories**. Textual lineage lists may retrospectively organize multiple generations. An artwork's stylistic resemblance does not prove the journey or nationality of an individual artisan. Monumental architectural precision does not authorize unsupported engineering legends. An early printing specimen's chronological priority requires care; do not assert an uncontested world-first based on later memory. Technical and intellectual forms existed within unequal conditions of elite access, labour, donation and limited source survival.
