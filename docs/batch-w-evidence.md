## Founding Joseon — sources, authority and limitations

1. [National Institute of Korean History (NIKH), Annals of Joseon](https://sillok.history.go.kr/main/main.do), royal histories from Taejo through Sejong. The relevant entries cover accession, succession disputes, institutions, royal offices and diplomatic acts. Compilation occurred after the events; later court politics, especially Taejong's victory, may inform portrayals of actors.
2. [NIKH, royal-annals compilation introductions](https://sillok.history.go.kr/intro/haejae.do). In particular, `Taejo sillok` was compiled after his death on Taejong's orders, rather than written as an unmediated daily diary by Taejo himself.
3. [NIKH, Taejong Annals preface](https://sillok.history.go.kr/id/kca_000). Its flattering biography of Yi Bang-won and portrayal of rivals are evidence of legitimation as well as possible details of his life.
4. [Academy of Korean Studies (AKS), Jeong Mong-ju](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0050190): late-Goryeo opposition to a change of dynasty and killing in 1392; Jeong's reformist agenda did not imply that he supported the transfer of the throne.
5. [NIKH, historical textbook account of Taejo and Sejong](https://contents.history.go.kr/front/ta/view.do?levelId=ta_p102r_0010_0010): retrospective educational framing. It cannot independently prove speeches, unanimity or motives in 1392.

**Major uncertainty and method:** Wihwa withdrawal motives and statements about rival princes were transmitted through partisan retrospective texts. The administrative implementation of land reform, hopae registration and the limitations on private forces were uneven. Ming recognition involved a hierarchy of diplomatic titles and practical negotiation, not direct bureaucratic annexation. Hanyang buildings standing today include later rebuilding, so original urban design must not be inferred from present appearance without dating. Families, provincial households and labourers are underrepresented in court records; general social mechanisms are not invented individual testimony.

## Seonjo to Injo — sources, authority and limitations

1. [NIKH, Annals and Gwanghaegun journals](https://sillok.history.go.kr/main/main.do). Use Seonjo, Gwanghaegun and Injo reign narratives for dated proposals and decisions, not as unbiased descriptions of factional blame. Gwanghaegun's record survives different compiled redactions; post-1623 legitimation shaped evaluation of his policies.
2. [AKS, Gwanghaegun](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0005335): postwar administration, 1609 Japanese diplomatic resumption, contested royal legitimacy and the 1619 Ming expedition. Contrasting judgements must not be flattened into one pro- or anti-Gwanghaegun school.
3. [AKS, Gang Hong-rip](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0001497): the Korean contingent at Sarhu, surrender/captivity and subsequent mediation in 1627. Biography and the allegation of a secret royal instruction require critical reading.
4. [AKS, Jianzhou observational account](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0076457): Yi Min-hwan's post-captivity `Geonju mun-gyeon-rok` and proposals for defence. This is a first-person source with an interest in defending its author's own conduct.
5. [NIKH, Yi Gwal rebellion chronology](https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/kc/view.do?levelId=kc_i305000): January–February 1624, rebel occupation of Seoul and Injo's flight to Gongju. Disappointment with rewards and security panic are distinct factors.
6. [AKS, Byeongja Horan](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0023151): December 1636–January 1637 Qing invasion, Namhansanseong and subsequent submission.
7. [AKS, Na Heon collection and siege diaries](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0011545): near-contemporary fortress, surrender and hostage-travel diaries. The writer's access and status limit the perspectives preserved.
8. [NIKH, Injo Annals entry, March 1623](https://sillok.history.go.kr/id/kpa_10103018_001): an example of the restoration's intervention into former royal-family ritual titles. Evidence of a new government's action, not universal public consent.

**Major uncertainty and method:** No single royal chronicle supplies an exhaustive count of post-1598 civilian losses or equal coverage of villages. Reported fiscal reform proposals require differentiation from implemented policies; the Gyeonggi Daedong system began in 1608, not nationwide immediately. The 1619 Sarhu surrender and the 1627 treaty's meaning were disputed; avoid projecting a modern neutral-state doctrine onto Gwanghaegun. The 1636–37 siege and postwar hostage experiences should be compared across official and firsthand records; royal submission did not end commoner captivity or economic disruption. Modern national humiliation symbolism should be distinguished from 1637 actions.

## Release qualification gates

The Batch W fixture pins exactly 301 prior article blobs and all 15 canonical Git blobs. The only old article modifications permitted are suffix-only blocks in the four enumerated IDs; old article metadata, original block objects and passage IDs remain identical. The regression checks 16 canonical newly authored IDs, evidence references, duplicate paragraphs, exactly two 10-stop journeys, search aliases, a 20,000–30,000-word delta, deterministic pack/shard output and public assets. CI runs exact-head generated-assets Chromium tests at 1440/390/320 pixels and real-origin browser tests against the **actual Cloudflare check-discovered branch URL**, not an invented alias. The tests intentionally scope PR path triggers to Batch W so their release-frozen historical fixture does not block later unrelated batches. Scholarly peer review, physical-device testing and independent audit of every historical claim are NOT_TESTED unless separately documented.
