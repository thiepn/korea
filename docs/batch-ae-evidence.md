## Part II.4 — Buyeo, Okjeo, Dongye, and the Northern Polities

**Institutional sources and textual traditions**

1. **National Institute of Korean History (NIKH)**, *Revised Korean History*, Buyeo formation, diplomacy, geography and political system: https://contents.history.go.kr/front/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_004_0040 . Expert secondary synthesis and discussion of the primary *Sanguozhi* (Book of Wei, Eastern Yi chapter), *Hou Hanshu*, historical chronology and archaeological correlations. Different sections address disputed founding dates, changes in royal centers and relations with frontier states.
2. **NIKH**, *Historical Sources of Korea*, *Buyeo: origins and establishment*: https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_003_0100 . Primary excerpts from *Shiji*, *Hou Hanshu* and later Korean traditions including *Samguk Yusa*, with editorial commentary. The *Shiji* mention demonstrates a historical source horizon, **not** a precisely dated founding act. Later Dongmyeong, Hae Mosu and North/East Buyeo stories require distinct source criticism.
3. **NIKH**, *Revised Korean History*, *Dongye and Okjeo*: https://contents.history.go.kr/front/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_004_0050 . Historical geography and critical treatment of locality, territorial customs, political status, labels and the *Sanguozhi* outsider account.
4. **NIKH**, *Historical Sources of Korea*, *Okjeo's location and transformation*: https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_003_0040 . Annotated Wei account clarifies the naming of Eastern Okjeo, uncertain differentiation of north/south, local `samno` leaders, Han Eastern Section Commandant, and the **debate** over Goguryeo indirect versus direct rule.
5. **NIKH**, *Dongye's chaekhwa territorial compensation*: https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/mid/hm_003_0070?tabId=r . Source and editorial interpretation describe village territorial practice, `eupgun`/`samno` hierarchies and community boundaries. It does **not** provide a universal legal code or establish modern private property.
6. **NIKH**, *Annotated primary source dossiers for the pre-Three Kingdoms period*: https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/list.do?periodCode=hm_age_10 . Identifies *Sanguozhi* reports on Yeonggo, Buyeo leaders, Dongye custom, Okjeo marriage and society, as well as later traditions for comparison.

**Interpretive limitations**

- The *Sanguozhi* is a third-century compilation by an observer serving a continental court. The *Hou Hanshu* was compiled later and may share records; agreement between them is not automatically independent corroboration. External ethnonyms, court titles and numbers were politically mediated.
- The date of a named source, the period it describes and the date a political custom began are separate. The *Shiji* passage's historical horizon and subsequent dating remain debated. The legendary biographies of founders are culturally significant **without supplying direct early dates**.
- Do not map a single stable national boundary onto diverse Songhua, Liao, Yalu, Tumen and northeastern coastal communities. A Han jurisdictional name does not prove direct uniform government over every village. A recorded Goguryeo tribute demand does not supply an annual tax table.
- The territorial rule `chaekhwa`, Dongye `Mucheon`, Buyeo `Yeonggo`, marriage forms and Okjeo funerary report are *reported practices*. They cannot be generalized unchanged across all households and centuries, nor converted into invented conversations, detailed liturgies or individual experiences.
- The label `Mohe` belongs primarily to other northern contexts and is not retroactively equated to every Buyeo/Okjeo/Dongye settlement. No archaeology-to-ethnicity identity is inferred solely from an artifact style.
- **Ownership:** II.4 presents the named northern communities, institutions, frontier interactions and historiographical identity problems. II.3 owns the detailed commandery administration and its separate history; III.1 and later Part III material own the Goguryeo royal chronology, interstate campaigns and internal institutions. I.6 owns broad prehistoric ethnogenesis, while II.4.10 owns only later memory of these northern groups.

## Part IV.6 — Balhae Society and Civilization

**Institutional sources, inscriptions, archaeology**

1. **NIKH**, Balhae (698/699–926), capital archaeological evidence and wider society: https://contents.history.go.kr/front/km/view.do?levelId=km_039_0070_0020_0020_0020 . Source for Shangjing Longquanfu urban phases, differing royal centers, archaeological excavation history and complex state geography. A capital is not a complete portrait of every rural settlement.
2. **Academy of Korean Studies (AKS)**, *Princess Jeonghye Tomb* (updated 2024): https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0051146 . Expert entry for the royal daughter's Dunhua stone chamber, death in **777**, interment in **780**, associated funerary inscription and sculptures. This is direct evidence for a named member of the royal family, not for rural status norms.
3. **AKS**, *Princess Jeonghyo Tomb*: https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0051192 . Expert entry on the fourth daughter of King Mun, death in **792**, the Longtou Mountain brick tomb, mural contexts and funerary commemoration. A royal monument is selective evidence of elite ideals and patronage, not the deceased's unfiltered autobiography.
4. **NIKH**, Balhae's rise, historical territories, urban culture, diplomacy and Buddhist sites: https://contents.history.go.kr/front/ta/view.do?levelId=ta_h51_0040_0020_0010 . Useful synthetic educational reference; simplifications about a fully uniform population or the precise reach of kingship are explicitly not treated as demonstrated.
5. **NIKH**, *Balhae development and the five capitals*: https://contents.history.go.kr/front/ta/view.do?levelId=ta_m51_0050_0020 . Another historical survey showing state links to Tang, Gojoseon/Goguryeo continuities, Buddhist work and landscape. Date and genre of each assertion are evaluated rather than copied as timeless fact.

**Interpretive limitations**

- Balhae was territorially and culturally heterogeneous. `Mohe/Malgal` was not a single unchanging group, and a government claim of continuity with Goguryeo cannot establish a demographic census. Neither ethnic exclusion nor a timeless national identity is demonstrated by one burial plan or an external court label.
- There is no complete Balhae household register, surviving national tax code, village literacy rate, merchant cargo ledger, monetary price series or regional manufacturing output census. Descriptions of everyday village economy, transport, workshop training, or gendered labor must be qualified as environmental and material **inference** unless direct evidence is identified.
- Tang and Japanese official written records supply invaluable evidence for diplomacy, literacy and relations but foreground their own interests. No claim of universal literacy or schooling follows from official letters or envoys.
- At Shangjing and other capitals, reused foundations, later repairs, excavations and differing levels of preservation limit exact phase attribution. The Jeonghye and Jeonghyo inscriptions are unusually precise royal observations yet **not** representative population data.
- The physical presence of Buddhist sites establishes patronage and religious practice somewhere, **not** the faith of all residents. Tang-style architecture, pottery and mortuary features may indicate local adaptation or exchange rather than direct foreign administration.
- Modern Korean, Chinese and Russian scholarship operate across present-day borders; dating, excavation methods and primary evidence decide claims, not the nationality of a researcher. Contestation is acknowledged without treating all claims as equally supported.
- **Ownership:** IV.5 has founding, rulers, administration, foreign relations and state collapse; IV.6 owns social diversity, family, settlements, food, crafts, roads and economic life, belief, writing, art, archaeology and historiography. IV.2 and IV.3 own Unified Silla government and everyday Silla life, not Balhae. IV.6.10 treats interpretive identity questions rather than duplicating IV.5 political chronology.
