## IV.1 — source basis

- **National Institute of Korean History**, historical chronology and synthesis of the Silla–Tang War, 670–676: https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/kc/view.do?code=kc_age_10&levelId=kc_i101220. Provides Maeso/Gibeolpo and changing military geography. Treats the settlement in an interpretive national-history frame; casualty figures and enumerated troops transmitted by later texts are not audited population data.
- **Academy of Korean Studies**, *Unification of the Three Kingdoms* (삼국통일): https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0026483. Contextual reference for Tang–Silla competition, Baekje integration and the territorial outcome. Later unity formulae are not evidence of modern ethnonational self-understanding or all-community consent.
- **Academy of Korean Studies**, *Goguryeo* (고구려): https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0003323. Former Goguryeo restoration movements, forced relocation and the variety of post-668 affiliations. Distinguishes displaced high-status leaders from undocumented commoners.
- **Academy of Korean Studies**, *Andong Protectorate* (안동도호부): https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0034607. Critical context: the named nine military governorates, forty-two prefectures and hundred counties express a proposed Tang political scheme; full implementation is disputed. Notes movement of the protectorate's seat and frontier limits.
- **Academy of Korean Studies**, *Three Kingdoms period*: https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0026481. Secondary source for the historiography of 삼한일통 and the selection of the three dynastic histories by medieval Korean compilers.

**Interpretive constraints:** Do not reopen the primary battle narrative belonging to III.10; Chapter IV.1 owns the consequences of conquest and the formation of postwar authority. Do not assign later mature Silla nine-province arrangements to the immediate 676 settlement. Do not imply Tang governed every administrative unit that it named on paper. Distinguish former Baekje regional adaptation, former Goguryeo movement, forced migration, Silla's southern realm, changing eastern/northeastern communities, and the specific formation of Balhae (primarily IV.5). Avoid retrojecting modern nationality, fixed borders or linguistic homogeneity. Do not turn a ceramic style or a dynastic chronicle into a witness to an individual household's allegiance.

## IX.2 — source basis

- **National Archives of Korea**, *Founding Constitution* (제헌헌법): https://www.archives.go.kr/next/newsearch/listSubjectDescription.do?id=001430&pageFlag=C&sitePage=1-2-2. Institutional chronology for May 10 election, Constituent Assembly and 1948 constitutional establishment. Formal procedure and legitimacy are not identical to unrestricted nationwide electoral access.
- **Republic of Korea government**, *Constitution and Government*: https://www.korea.net/Government/Constitution-and-Government/Constitution. Retrospective official account of 17 July promulgation, 20 July selection of President Rhee and 15 August inauguration. The government is an interested constitutional narrator, not a neutral survey of political opponents.
- **Jeju 4.3 Peace Foundation**, detailed event history and civilian harms: https://jeju43peace.or.kr/kor/sub01_01_03.do. Documents both the 3 April armed attacks and the extraordinarily destructive counterinsurgency. Its victim totals require the original defined period, investigative method and category; Jeju and Yeosu–Suncheon must be differentiated.
- **Republic of Korea official commemoration**, official acknowledgement of Jeju civilian abuses: https://www.korea.net/Government/Briefing-Room/Presidential-Speeches/view?articleId=196683. Useful for later public memory and acknowledgement, not a direct eyewitness source for every 1948 event.
- **National Institute of Korean History**, *1946 North Korean Land Reform Decree*: https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_157_0010. Primary published text identifying confiscation, exceptions, cultivator grants and village committees. Legal intent differs from verified implementation and individual ownership outcomes.
- **Wilson Center**, research report using Soviet occupation documents: https://www.wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/korean-uprisings-1946-was-us-occupation-responsible-korean-war. Counterpoint to an assumption that northern people's committees were homogeneous or that land reform was already fully planned at liberation; Soviet records are interested political observations.
- **US Department of State, FRUS 1948**, report of September Soviet withdrawal communication: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1948v06/d894. Contemporary diplomatic transmission of Soviet official claim; differentiate announcement, actual withdrawal and continuing advisory connections.
- **US Department of State, FRUS 1949**, NSC assessment of withdrawal, South Korean institutions and risks: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1949v07p2/d209. Primary American policy document whose judgments about Korean legitimacy, Soviet motivation and military readiness represent an actor's evaluation, not an objective description.
- **US Department of State, FRUS 1948**, December withdrawal debate: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1948v06/d928. Specific evidence of changes to the planned US withdrawal schedule.
- **US Presidential Library**, Truman's explanation of the withdrawal decision: https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/public-papers/310/statement-president-decision-withdraw-us-forces-korea-1947-1949. Retrospective defense of US governmental decision, to be compared with contemporary files.

**Interpretive constraints:** The May 1948 election covered only accessible southern constituencies and the north refused UN commission access; it cannot be labeled a free all-Korea vote. The Republic and DPRK both made peninsula-wide sovereignty claims without effective control beyond their zones. Do not treat multiple northern party labels as proof of unrestricted pluralist competition; distinguish formal constitutions and lived coercion in both states. No false symmetry of armed rebellion and state responsibility for mass civilian killing. The opening assault on 25 June 1950 and detailed combat belong to IX.3, while IX.2.10 owns the prewar calculations and responsibility for the decision to launch the invasion. The historical responsibility to examine Soviet support and Kim Il Sung's plans does not eliminate local border skirmishes, southern unification rhetoric or US strategic choices.
