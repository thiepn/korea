## 2. Jinhan and Saro/Silla formation: sources and contested evidence

1. **Academy of Korean Studies (AKS)**, *Samhan*, https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0026922 . Specialist reference to external third-century `Sanguozhi` Wei `Dongyi` accounts, Jinhan and Byeonhan each listed with twelve polities, and limits of their archaeology and geography. `Guo` in these lists must not be assumed to mean a mature territorial state with identical offices in each settlement.
2. **AKS**, *Saro-guk*, https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0025526 . Discusses Saro as a Jinhan polity, the later political expansion into Silla and the historiographical distinction between 57 BCE traditional accession and externally attested early-polity setting. The entry differentiates compiled narratives from an independently attested founder biography.
3. **National Institute of Korean History (NIKH)**, *Foundation of Silla*, https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/kc/view.do?code=kc_age_10&levelId=kc_i101210 . The 57 BCE traditional foundation, Saro's gradual rise, fourth-century diplomatic horizon, Wolseong construction and large mound-burial development are discussed as different evidential layers. The traditional Hyeokgeose chronology is **not** a direct archaeological construction date.
4. **AKS**, *Geungi-guk*, https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0007393 . Regional location disputed between different later place-name identifications; illustrates why phonetic similarity to one modern locality is insufficient historical proof.
5. **AKS**, *Horo-guk*, https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0063741 , and *Juseon-guk*, https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0053314 . Chinese-character transcriptions offer polity names, not secure exact coordinates or precise reconstructions of indigenous-language forms. Modern mapping must make these limits clear.

**Method and boundaries:**
- Jinhan was a plural political landscape, not one centrally administered nation identical to later Silla. A `Sanguozhi` polity name, a local pottery group and a `Samguk sagi` royal name are not interchangeable evidence types.
- **Samguk sagi** was compiled in the twelfth century, long after the putative 57 BCE accession of **Park Hyeokgeose**; its six-villages story, Al-yeong, Park/Seok/Kim lines and sacred foundation motifs are central to later political memory but their earliest details are not independently verified contemporary records.
- The Gyeongju basin, lower rivers, sea routes, productive valleys and fortification sites supported changing political possibilities; they do not establish one inevitable center or allow undated walls and graves to be attributed to founder-era kings.
- Large Gyeongju stone-mound wooden-chamber burials reflect **later** concentrated wealth and social differentiation, not unchanged first-century BCE funerary or bureaucratic conditions. Burial form and import goods do not directly prove ethnic identity, succession, office or jurisdiction.
- **II.7 owns** Jinhan source problems, physical setting, regional polities, Saro formation, early royal origin memory, clan contest, formative economy/ritual, neighbors and the threshold into later Silla. **II.6** owns western Mahan/Baekje emergence; **II.8** owns Byeonhan/Gaya; **II.3** the northern commanderies; **III.1–III.5** own later Silla kingship, campaigns and bureaucratic law. Do not duplicate post-668 Unified Silla institutions of **IV.2**.

## 3. Goryeo's pre-Mongol foreign world: primary-reference and specialist anchors

1. **NIKH**, *Seo Hui's negotiations*, https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_048_0020 . Annotated passage from `Goryeosa` on the **993** Liao invasion, Seo Hui and Xiao Sunning, the Goguryeo legacy argument, and creation/consolidation of the **six eastern Yalu counties**. Transmitted speeches are not stenographic eyewitness transcripts.
2. **NIKH**, *Goryeo–Khitan diplomatic relations*, https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_048_0090 . Source criticism of wars **993, 1010, 1018–19**, the **1019 Gwiju** battle and sustained contact after that conflict up to Liao's defeat in 1125. Liao/Goryeo were not in uninterrupted warfare.
3. **NIKH**, *Gwiju victory*, https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/kc/view.do?code=kc_age_20&levelId=kc_i200400 . Specific narrative for Gang Gam-chan, campaign commanders, Heunghwajin and retreating enemy army in 1018–19. Traditional large army/casualty figures are attributed to the transmitted record, not independently audited headcounts; the water-releasing ambush is a **reported tactical episode**, not the basis for speculative hydrological calculations.
4. **NIKH**, *Nine northeastern fortresses*, https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_048_0060 and *Yun Gwan campaign*, https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/kc/view.do?code=kc_age_20&levelId=kc_i201250 . Wanyan expansion, **1104** defeats, recruitment of Byeolmuban (mounted, foot and monastic divisions), **1107** fortress campaign and **1109** relinquishment. Exact locations of the fortresses remain disputed (including Hamhung-versus-more-northerly reconstructions); they must not be assigned invented fixed modern coordinates.
5. **NIKH**, *Jin's foundation and Goryeo's foreign policy*, https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_048_0110 . Jin's creation **1115**, fall of Liao **1125**, Goryeo's disputed decision to recognize Jin imperial precedence **1126**, and the connected Song crisis. The rhetoric of kinship in diplomatic messages does not establish modern ethnic descent; courtly submission is not synonymous with direct foreign administration.
6. **NIKH**, *Goryeo–Song diplomacy*, https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_048_0080 . Goryeo embassy **962**, altered relations after 993, reopening in **1071**, major court reception **1078**, and **Xu Jing's 1123** observations. Private merchants and book transmission continued across periods of official diplomatic interruption.
7. **NIKH**, *Seonjong and scholarly exchange*, https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/kc/view.do?code=kc_age_20&levelId=kc_n202400 . **Uicheon's 1085 journey to Song**, rare books and text collection. A travel account or book request cannot establish universal literacy or exact adoption of every received practice.
8. **NIKH**, *Song perceptions of Goryeo*, https://contents.history.go.kr/front/km/print.do?levelId=km_030_0040_0020_0010&whereStr= . Song official factions' changing incentives, private commerce even when formal envoys were absent, and asymmetry of foreign eyewitness observation.
9. **AKS**, *Byeongnando*, https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0022829 . International river port on the Yesong River near Gaegyeong, visited by Song merchants and other foreign traders. Exact continuous shipment totals, individual itineraries and population census are not documented; some later merchant-origin claims require qualification.
10. **NIKH**, *Goryeo's foreign relations and Song economy*, https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/ta/view.do?levelId=ta_m61_0060_0010 . Useful introductory synthesis of exchange and political hierarchy, not an independent substitute for dated documents above.

**Boundaries and uncertainty:**
- **V.7** owns the pre-Mongol interstate system, campaigns and agreements with Khitan Liao, Jurchen and Jin, Song and Japan, border institutions and foreign merchant contact. **V.9**, not V.7, owns the Mongol/Yuan conquest and imperial system. **V.4** owns Goryeo's domestic economy, **V.6** owns arts/scholarship/technical production, **V.8** owns detailed domestic military-rule crises (including Yi Ja-gyeom's later political significance). Pre-918 maritime Silla/Jang Bogo belongs to **IV.7**.
- Period-specific diplomatic words such as investiture, tribute and subordination signal court rank and material exchange but are not automatically modern full annexation, constitutional submission, or a treaty between legally equal nation-states.
- The `Goryeosa` is a later compilation; court speeches, reported troop numbers, "family" language and source lists of goods are attributed to their transmission, never presented as independently verified audio or measurement.
- Goryeo's borders were formed through settlement, fortification and continuing relationships with Jurchen communities, not one timeless precise line. Those communities possessed interests, local politics and agency.
- Japan/Tsushima connections require chronological care: do not import mature later Joseon permits, ports or pirate-conflict institutions into eleventh-century Goryeo. The surviving Japan-specific evidence is thinner than the documented NIKH Khitan/Jin/Song material; parts of the V.7.8 interpretation are **structural, qualified comparative inference**, not independently verified individual voyages.
